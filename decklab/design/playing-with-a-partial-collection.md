# Building decks with a partially recorded collection

Design discussion, 20 November 2026.

Matt identified keeping Deck Lab in sync with the physical cards as its biggest pain point. He suspects the work of entering and maintaining a collection puts off people he has invited to try it. Their reasons still need to be heard directly. The design question is whether someone who never intends to catalogue their loose cards can still use Deck Lab to decide what to pull or buy for their next game.

Deck Lab already lets someone start a deck from a pasted list or a precon, deal a practice hand, and record loose cards in small batches. The difficulty comes when the Cards tab labels a copy “missing” and offers to copy that shortage to a shop list. An unrecorded copy could be sitting in the player's drawer. A quicker scanner reduces entry time, but every unscanned box and unrecorded trade still affects how much the player can trust that list. ## A checklist to try at home

**Decks → Check cards for a deck** implements the first part of this idea. Each profile can keep one private checklist, give it an optional name, and reopen it after saving. [How to use the checklist](../README.md#check-cards-for-a-deck) · [Story E5-S43](stories.md#e5-s43)

A player is considering eight upgrades for Commander night. On their phone, they give the deck a name and enter the eight cards. They can leave the rest of the deck and the collection blank.

1. All eight start under **Check at home**. The player can work through the rows as they look in their box.
2. The player finds four cards in a box and marks them **Set aside**. This records the copies physically pulled for this deck. The player can leave the printing and total owned unknown.
3. The player chooses **Buy** for two cards and leaves the last two to check later. At the shop they see **2 to buy · 2 still to check · 4 set aside**. They can copy the two they want or correct a choice if they find another copy.

Picking a card for an upgrade expresses intent. Pulling one copy establishes that the player has set it aside for this build. That distinction matters if two decks want the same card, or a 60-card deck needs three copies and the player finds only one. Each choice belongs to the checklist. The Cards tab still offers its deck-level copy action based on recorded inventory. [Member deck and buying views](../README.md#peoples-own-collections-spaces)

## Other ways to reduce upkeep

- **Track just the decks on the shelf.** Let someone treat three sleeved decks as current and leave loose boxes uncounted. “One copy in your Elf deck. Loose cards unchecked” gives them somewhere to look. Players who do keep a complete inventory can continue to use calculated shortages.
- **Start with the physical deck as it is.** For a modified precon, use the stock list as a starting point and record the swaps the player remembers. For a homebrew with no list, let the player name the deck and record today's first swap. Both paths give them a useful history from today. ManaBox also distinguishes registering a deck already in hand from building one with recorded cards. [ManaBox physical-deck guide](https://www.manabox.app/guides/decks/collection-decks/)
- **Record a physical change once.** Moving a card between two tracked decks could update both lists in one action. Ticking off cards brought home from the shop could reuse the list that went there.
- **Make it easy to return after a gap.** A player who missed three months of swaps could check one deck against what's in its box today and resume from there. Reconstructing the intervening trades can stay optional.

## How to test the idea

Ask someone who passed on Deck Lab, “Last time you changed a deck, how did you figure out which cards you needed to buy?” Then try the checklist with a change they are actually considering. Watch whether they can take it to their box or the shop, whether they understand what is still unchecked, and whether they reach for it on their next deck change. Their experience can guide the next change.
